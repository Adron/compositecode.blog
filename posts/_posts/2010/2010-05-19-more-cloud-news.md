Recently [Amazon](http://www.amazon.com) jumped into the relational database cloud competition with [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com).  Up until the [6th of this month](http://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2010/05/06/announcing-amazon-relational-database-service-rds-in-the-northern-california-region/), [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com) had the only cloud with a real dedicated relational database offering in [SQL Azure](http://www.microsoft.com/windowsazure/sqlazure/).  Now [Amazon](http://www.amazon.com) has their [Relational Database Service](http://aws.amazon.com/rds/) heating up the competition.

In other news, [Google](http://www.google.com) finally joined the storage party with their recent [launch announcement](http://techcrunch.com/2010/05/18/google-to-launch-amazon-s3-competitor-google-storage-at-io/) at the [I/O Conference](http://code.google.com/events/io/2010/).  So now we have [Amazon](http://www.amazon.com), [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com), and [Google](http://www.google.com) as the big companies on the block throwing down on the storage offerings.  Stay tuned for more!

Currently I am working on a code kata putting together ideas from [Roy Osherove](http://weblogs.asp.net/rosherove/)‘s [The Art of Unit Testing](http://www.artofunittesting.com/) and what one needs to know for testing in enterprise environments, abstracting the appropriate code to take into account web services, files, I/O, architectural issues, and other elements of coding.
