![](/images/adronhall.smugmug.com/Software/Sharepoint/sharepoint2010andanalytics/logo/817640842_mjTv4-S.png)

[Sharepoint 2010](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) has some great new features.  Especially considering some of the disturbing necessities of previous [Sharepoint](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) Versions.

![](/images/adronhall.smugmug.com/Software/Sharepoint/sharepoint2010andanalytics/1269363457Real-One/817640787_FFDM5-Th.png)The first thing I noticed was that [Visual Studio 2010](http://www.microsoft.com/visualstudio/en-us/pre-order-visual-studio-2010?CR_CC=100340688&WT.mc_id=SEARCH&WT.srch=1&CR_SCC=100340688) has default templates for building [Sharepoint](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) Projects.  This is huge compared to the monstrous and annoying effort required for development on previous versions.

Being able to build [Sharepoint](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) Projects from [Visual Studio 2010](http://www.microsoft.com/visualstudio/en-us/pre-order-visual-studio-2010?CR_CC=100340688&WT.mc_id=SEARCH&WT.srch=1&CR_SCC=100340688) is a huge change, enabling IT & Development Groups to actually use regular Windows 7 Machines instead of needing Windows Server operating systems and licenses to build [Sharepoint](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) Applications.  To me, this was always a non-starter, and actively discouraged [Sharepoint](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) Applications in the past ? obviously most companies had not listened to the voice of reason.  But now, I have changed to actively encouraging [Sharepoint 2010](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) Projects now.

![](/images/adronhall.smugmug.com/Software/Sharepoint/sharepoint2010andanalytics/1269363482connecttonetwork/817640785_muM25-Th.png)

The second thing is, it is more open and easier to build public facing sites finally.  This has been desperately needed.  I am glad that it has finally become a higher priority.  Not that I totally trust the [Sharepoint 2010](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) Server to be fully web compliant, I have seen that they’ve taken leaps ahead.

The other thing I stumbled upon recently that I had not seen was the [analytics capabilities](http://blogs.msdn.com/ecm/archive/2010/03/21/introducing-web-analytics-in-sharepoint-2010.aspx) of [Sharepoint 2010](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com).  The overview is that you can get traffic, search, and inventory reports based on how much traffic the site gets, who visits the sites, and other web analytics data.  Definitely check out the blog entry "[Introducing Web Analytics in Sharepoint 2010](http://blogs.msdn.com/ecm/archive/2010/03/21/introducing-web-analytics-in-sharepoint-2010.aspx)" on the [Enterprise Content Management (WCM) Blog](http://blogs.msdn.com/ecm/default.aspx).

[![](/images/adronhall.smugmug.com/Software/Sharepoint/sharepoint2010andanalytics/clipimage001thumb/817659690_F8x5A-S.jpg)](http://blogs.msdn.com/ecm/archive/2010/03/21/introducing-web-analytics-in-sharepoint-2010.aspx)Between the [Sharepoint](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) Analytics, [Silverlight](http://www.microsoft.com/silverlight), and offerings we provide at [Webtrends](http://www.webtrends.com/) we now have direct extensibility points form the internal [Sharepoint](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) Data Sources, Analytics Data Stores, temp caching and other control mechanisms within [Silverlight](http://www.microsoft.com/silverlight), and much more.  What does all that techno-babble mean?  It means more data, more available faster, easier access, decreased development times, more accurate reporting, more timely reporting, and the list goes on.  Pretty much ever metric about web analytics within [Sharepoint 2010](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) has just gotten better.

The other architectural pieces I noticed is the [Sharepoint](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) Aggregation of data is now per web application in the farm, per site collection, per site, and per search service application.  This can be broken out even further with On Demand [Webtrends](http://www.webtrends.com/) Analytics by providing ETL between the [Sharepoint](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) and pulling the [Sharepoint](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) or extended analytics data [Webtrends](http://www.webtrends.com/) provides into the Business Intelligence (BI) framework within an enterprise.  At this point I can only imagine the scale and available data points for managing enterprise collaboration, social media, and other capabilities with this combination.  I’m looking forward to seeing what kinds of ROI we can see from these integrations, whatever it is, I already know it will be measurable and I suspect substantial.

For more information about [Sharepoint 2010](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) check out the [Sharepoint Blog](http://blogs.msdn.com/sharepoint/) and for more information regarding how [Webtrends](http://www.webtrends.com/) can get your [Sharepoint](http://sharepoint2010.microsoft.com) Site Analytics extended check out [Webtrends Professional Services](http://www.webtrends.com/Products/Services)!
