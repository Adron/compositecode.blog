As many in the analytics industry know, [Adobe](http://www.adobe.com) has [put up](http://www.omniture.com/press/777) [$1.8 billion](http://www.adobe.com/aboutadobe/invrelations/adobeandomniture.html) for [Omniture](http://www.omniture.com).  Several blog entries floating around out there;  [More color on Adobe + Omniture](http://blog.webanalyticsdemystified.com/weblog/2009/09/more-color-on-adobe-omniture.html) by Eric Peterson, [Webtrends encouraged by Adobe?s purchase of Omniture](http://blog.oregonlive.com/siliconforest/2009/09/webtrends_take_on_adobes_purch.html) at the Oregon Live Site, [Congrats to the Omniture folks! A Q & A with myself](http://www.multichannelmetrics.com/congrats-omniture-co-a-qa-with-myself/) by Akin, [Why Did Adobe Buy Omniture?](http://webanalysis.blogspot.com/2009/09/why-did-adobe-buy-omniture.html) by Anil Batra, [Adobe buys Omniture:  What are they thinking?](http://semphonic.blogs.com/semangel/2009/09/adobe-buys-omniture-what-are-they-thinking.html) by SemAngel (Gary Angel) and many others.

Amid all the things written in those articles and entries, I will leave just as they are written without comment.  Everything written in these conversations are pretty much similar ideas, about how it is truly an odd purchase.  That is great from my perspective, I am a [Webtrends](http://www.webtrends.com) Employee and I enjoy the work very much.  I find the competition growing and lively.  But there is one thing I want to comment on, and it is mostly a technical observation that I would love for non-technical people to take heed of.

The one thing I want to comment on is this mythic idea [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com) may be or should be scooping in at the last second and bidding on [Omniture](http://www.omniture.com).  There was even some statements that [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com) should have bought [Omniture](http://www.omniture.com).  The major issue with this is two fold;  [Omniture](http://www.omniture.com) & [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com) are built, generally, on completely different technology stacks.  [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com) uses, well, [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com)‘s Technology Stack and [Omniture](http://www.omniture.com) uses a deluge of non-[Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com) Technology (with a few MS Products here and there, like their Excel Tools).  So even if that was a match, it would have been really bad for [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com), as the publicity for not dog fooding their own technology after this mythic purchase would have been huge.  The cost for [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com) to rewrite the entirety of almost every tool [Omniture](http://www.omniture.com) has would have been overwhelming, even for [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com).  The cost, in the end, would absolutely not be worth it for [Microsoft](http://www.microsoft.com).

I won’t venture about our technology stack at [Webtrends](http://www.webtrends.com), nor [Adobe](http://www.adobe.com) & [Omniture](http://www.omniture.com)‘s Stack, other than to say from the technical perspective [Adobe](http://www.adobe.com) matches [Omniture](http://www.omniture.com)‘s Tech Stack far better than a Microsoft & Omniture stack.  Adobe has a fine history of taking disparate ? specifically non-Microsoft technologies ? and getting them to work together.

…and to a great 09′ and improving economy for everyone, Cheers!
