I’m kind of surprised, I didn’t even realize that IBM has made some inroads into [cloud computing](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_computing).  I however shouldn’t have been at all surprised, as IBM has made great changes over the years.  Their offering doesn’t seem as well laid out as [AWS](http://aws.amazon.com) or [Azure](http://www.microsoft.com/windowsazure) but it is a significant presence just being IBM.

[IBM Cloud Computing](http://www.ibm.com/ibm/cloud/) looks like most of the other things the company does these days, which is primarily services based.  I don’t mean web services either, even though I’m sure they use those, but literally services based versus offering actual products, hardware, or some type of applications.  They do have their [iNotes](https://www.lotuslive.com/en/services/inotes) and other tools like that in the cloud.  Otherwise it isn’t immediately obvious how their cloud compares to [Azure](http://www.microsoft.com/windowsazure), [AWS](http://aws.amazon.com), [vmware](http://www.vmware.com/solutions/cloud-computing/) or [Force.com](https://www.salesforce.com/platform/).  IBM’s website for cloud computing seems as disorganized as vmware’s or Force.com’s, which make [Azure](http://www.microsoft.com/windowsazure) and [AWS](http://aws.amazon.com) seem super well put together and structured.  With clearly defined features and tools for developing in their respective clouds.

Overall the services look attractive if IBM is already a provider of other services.  Kind of like [Azure](http://www.microsoft.com/windowsazure) looks more attractive to the .NET Stack of Developers and [AWS](http://aws.amazon.com) looks more attractive to PHP or Java Developers.  Even though both [Azure](http://www.microsoft.com/windowsazure) and [AWS](http://aws.amazon.com) support .NET, Java, PHP, and more, just from the IaaS or PaaS perspective though.

This begs the questions:

- What are the differences between the IBM Cloud Computing Services, Azure, AWS, Force.com, vmware, and others?
- Besides IBM, Azure, AWS, Force.com, and vmware, what other cloud services are really doing it right and pushing forward with redundancy, uptime, compute capabilities, and other key features?
- Anyone guessing on consolidations in the future with the current big players (AWS, Azure, Force.com)?
